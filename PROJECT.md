## 1. Website Audit — Current State

The current site lives on a legacy hosted-storefront platform (URL pattern `/cswllc/shop/home` is Network Solutions–era ecommerce). Even without direct fetch access, the structural and SEO problems are visible from cached snippets and competitor comparisons:

**What's broken**

- **No clear hero promise.** The homepage opens with a product catalog, not a story. A first-time visitor cannot tell in 5 seconds *what they get* or *why pick Cumberland over a $15 online tee shop.*
- **Brand is the hero, not the customer.** Copy describes the company ("we offer," "we provide," "we have been in business since 2003") instead of the customer's win.
- **Problem is never named.** Nowhere does the site say "tired of waiting weeks for a sign company that never calls back?" or "school fundraiser next month and no shirts yet?" — the pains it solves go unspoken.
- **No 3-step plan.** Visitors don't see a path: how do I actually get a sign / shirt / wrap from these folks?
- **Weak/multiple CTAs.** Catalog-driven design = paralysis. There's no single "Start Your Project" button repeated throughout.
- **No stakes, no success vision.** Site doesn't paint the cost of bad signage or the win of a packed game day in matching shirts.
- **20 years of trust + 4.7★ / 95 reviews are buried.** Their single biggest moat (local reputation, fast turnaround, rush-friendly) isn't on the homepage.
- **Jargon-light but generic.** "Screen printing, digital printing, banners" — same words every competitor uses. No language that owns a position in the customer's mind.
- **Tech debt.** Legacy storefront = slow, no schema markup, weak local SEO, hard to edit, no mobile-first design, no IG integration, no reviews module.

**Audit scorecard**

| Element | Score (1–5) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hero clarity | 2 | Catalog-first, no headline promise |
| Customer-as-hero | 1 | All "we" copy |
| Problem articulation | 1 | Pain points unnamed |
| Guide signals (empathy + authority) | 2 | 20yr history + reviews exist but aren't surfaced |
| Plan clarity | 1 | No 3-step path |
| CTA strength | 2 | No dominant action |
| Stakes | 1 | Failure unmentioned |
| Success vision | 1 | No transformation painted |
| Local SEO posture | 2 | Crossville / Cumberland County not owned in copy |
| Mobile + speed | 2 | Legacy platform, not modern |

**Bottom line:** Cumberland has the *reputation* to dominate the Upper Cumberland Plateau market, but the website is bleeding leads to online printers and scrappier local competitors because it doesn't tell a story. We fix that.

## 2. SB7 BrandScript

### Character — Who is the customer-hero, and what do they want?

Cumberland serves two hero archetypes. The BrandScript runs both in parallel:

**Hero A — "The Coach / Chair / Pastor / Principal."**
The person on the hook to deliver shirts for the team, banners for the festival, signs for the fundraiser, hats for the church youth group. They're a volunteer or a stretched staffer. They want the order to be **right, on time, and not embarrassing.**

**Hero B — "The Local Owner."**
The shop owner, contractor, real-estate agent, restaurant operator who needs storefront signage, a wrapped truck, branded crew tees, yard signs, or window graphics — the visible face of their business. They want signage that **looks legit and gets them noticed.**

What they *both* want: **to look prepared, professional, and proud of how their team / business / event shows up.**

### Problem — What's standing in their way?

**External (the visible problem):**
- They need signs, shirts, banners, wraps, or branded gear by a deadline.
- Online printers won't do rush, won't stand behind quality, and don't help with art.
- Other local shops don't return calls, miss dates, or can't do everything in one place.

**Internal (the feeling):**
- Anxious — *"What if these don't show up in time?"*
- Embarrassed — *"What if the colors are off / spelling is wrong / it looks cheap?"*
- Overwhelmed — *"I don't know what artwork file you need, what shirt blank to choose, or what a wrap costs."*

**Philosophical (why it's wrong):**
- A small business, school, team, or church shouldn't have to settle for cheap-looking branding or get ghosted by their print shop. **Good people doing real work in their community deserve their signage and apparel to look as professional as they are.**

### The Guide — How Cumberland shows up

**Empathy.** *"We get it. You've got a season opener / grand opening / fundraiser / launch day, and the last thing you need is a print shop that flakes. We've watched coaches sweat over jerseys and owners stress over their first storefront sign for 23 years. We don't want that for you."*

**Authority.**
- 23 years in Crossville (since 2003).
- 4.7★ across 95+ reviews — *known* for rush orders, friendly handling, swift turnaround.
- Full in-house: screen printing, embroidery, vinyl, digital, signage, vehicle wraps — one shop, one PO, one point of contact.
- Hundreds of local schools, teams, churches, and small businesses already using us.

### The Plan — One simple 3-step process

1. **Tell us what you need.** Send a sketch, a logo, a Pinterest pin, or just talk it out — call, message, or fill the form.
2. **We design + quote — fast.** Free proof, clear price, honest timeline. No corporate run-around.
3. **You get it on time, looking right.** Delivered, ready to wear, ready to install.

### Call to Action

**Direct CTA:** *Start Your Project* (single dominant button, repeated everywhere)
**Transitional CTA:** *Get Our Free "Sign + Apparel Project Checklist"* (PDF lead magnet — see §6)

### Failure — Stakes if they don't act

- Showing up to game day in mismatched, faded shirts ordered late from a website.
- A storefront sign that looks like every other strip-mall vinyl banner — invisible.
- A wrapped truck driving around with the wrong shade of your brand color.
- Missing the fundraiser deadline because the online shop "lost the order."
- Worse: spending the same money for noticeably worse work.

### Success — The vision after they hire Cumberland

- The team runs out of the tunnel in clean, sharp, matching uniforms — phones up, photos everywhere.
- The new storefront sign stops cars in the lot. People say *"Whoa, that looks great."*
- The wrapped service truck *is* the marketing — every job site becomes a billboard.
- The pastor opens the email: proof approved, hats delivered, kids are pumped for camp.
- The owner walks in, points at the shirts on her crew, and says, "Yeah, that's us."

## 7. Build Plan — Astro + Tailwind + MDX + Decap CMS

### 7.1 Why this stack fits Cumberland

- **Astro** ships nearly-zero JS by default → blazing-fast static pages. Local-business sites *live or die* on Lighthouse + Google local pack ranking; Astro wins both.
- **Tailwind** lets us iterate visual variants quickly without cascading CSS debt.
- **MDX** for service pages — owner / staff can edit copy alongside structured component blocks (image rows, testimonials, CTAs) without breaking layouts.
- **Decap CMS** (formerly Netlify CMS) gives the shop a free, Git-backed admin UI at `/admin` so non-technical staff can update services, portfolio, and reviews without touching code.

### 7.2 Repo + Tooling

```
cumberland-signworks/
├── src/
│   ├── components/        Astro + Tailwind components (Hero, ThreeStepPlan, Stakes, ProofWall, CTA)
│   ├── content/           Astro Content Collections
│   │   ├── services/      MDX per service
│   │   ├── portfolio/     MDX per project (with hero image, tags)
│   │   └── testimonials/  MDX or JSON
│   ├── layouts/
│   ├── pages/
│   └── styles/
├── public/
│   ├── admin/             Decap CMS config
│   └── images/            optimized via Astro Image
├── astro.config.mjs
├── tailwind.config.cjs
└── package.json
```

**Plugins / Integrations:**
- `@astrojs/tailwind`
- `@astrojs/mdx`
- `@astrojs/sitemap`
- `@astrojs/image` (or `astro:assets`) for responsive `<Image>`
- `astro-seo` for structured data + OG tags
- `decap-cms-app` mounted at `/admin`
- `@astrolib/seo` for JSON-LD `LocalBusiness` schema

### 7.3 Forms & quote intake

The single most important interaction. Recommend:

- **Form provider:** Formspree, Basin, or self-hosted Cloudflare Worker → posts to email + Notion/Airtable for the team.
- **Anti-spam:** Cloudflare Turnstile (free, no UX hit).
- **Fields (kept short — 6 max):**
  1. Project type (multi-select: apparel / signs / wraps / print)
  2. Quantity / size (free text)
  3. When do you need it?
  4. Upload artwork (optional, accepts JPG/PNG/PDF/AI)
  5. Name + business / org
  6. Best phone or email
- **Auto-reply:** instant "We got it — Mike or Sarah will be in touch within 24 hours" (sets the *fast* expectation right after submit).
- **Routing:** webhook → Slack channel + email so no lead waits for someone to log into a portal.

### 7.4 Local SEO — the real ROI lever

This is where Astro pays for itself. Bake in:

- `LocalBusiness` JSON-LD on every page with Crossville address, phone, hours, geo, sameAs to Facebook/IG/Google.
- One landing page per **service × geography** combo for the top intent queries: e.g., `/signs/crossville-tn`, `/vehicle-wraps/cumberland-county`, `/apparel/upper-cumberland`. Each is a real page with real copy, not doorway spam.
- Embed Google Map on Contact page.
- Pull live Google reviews via Place API (or static rebuild nightly via GitHub Action).
- Pull Instagram via the official Graph API on a nightly build → static HTML cards (avoids JS-heavy embeds and stays fast).

### 7.5 Performance & accessibility budget

- Lighthouse ≥ 95 on every metric on mobile.
- Largest Contentful Paint < 1.8s.
- All images served via Astro Image with `loading="lazy"`, AVIF/WebP fallbacks.
- WCAG 2.1 AA color contrast.
- Click-to-call `tel:` link on the mobile sticky bar — every page.

### 7.6 Hosting & ops

- **Host:** Cloudflare Pages or Netlify (both free tier, both fine — Cloudflare slightly faster + better DDoS).
- **CI/CD:** GitHub → auto-deploy on push to `main`. Preview deploys for every PR.
- **Domain:** keep `cumberlandsignworks.com`; redirect old `/cswllc/shop/*` URLs → new pages with 301s to preserve any backlink equity.
- **Monitoring:** Cloudflare Web Analytics (privacy-friendly, no cookie banner needed) + Sentry for form errors.
- **Backups:** Git is the backup. Decap commits straight to repo.

## 10. Open Decisions Before Build Kicks Off

These don't block the BrandScript but should be locked before Phase 2:

1. **One hero brand color.** Recommend something that doesn't fight with red/blue/black (most competitors use those). A confident orange or a deep forest green would let Cumberland visually own a lane.
2. **Photo budget.** Half-day shoot vs full-day with on-location shots. Recommend full-day — 6+ months of social content + permanent web library.
3. **Online ordering — phase 2 or never?** You chose quote-driven for v1, smart. If phase 2 adds simple e-comm for branded-merch repeat customers (re-orders for school spirit shops, etc.), Astro + Snipcart or Shopify Lite slots in cleanly.
4. **Reviews source of truth.** Google + Birdeye + Facebook all have reviews. Consolidate display via one widget, or rebuild nightly via API and serve static.
5. **Owner / team bios on About page** — yes/no? Strongly recommend yes. Real faces close local deals.
